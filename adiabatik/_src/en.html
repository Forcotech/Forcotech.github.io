The challenge

On hot days, everything comes together

Air-cooled chillers work at their limit when the outdoor air is at its warmest. That is exactly when the fans run at full speed – and the unit is at its loudest.

  • High pressure: At 35 °C and above, condensing pressure rises. The unit throttles back or trips on fault.
  • Capacity: Cooling capacity and efficiency drop just when demand is highest.
  • Noise: Full fan speed means the highest sound level, often on warm summer nights too.
  • Space: There is usually no room left on site for a separate pre-cooling system.
How it works

The enclosure pre-cools the intake air

The acoustic enclosure already guides the air through a defined intake area. That is where the adiabatic module sits: water evaporates on a wetted surface and draws heat from the incoming air before it reaches the unit’s coil.

Evaporation

Cooling without refrigerant

Evaporating water lowers the air temperature. At 35 °C and 30 % relative humidity, approx. 6–9 K of cooling is possible.

Hygiene

Once-through, no spray nozzles

Water runs over the evaporation surface once: no recirculation, no standing water, no spray mist.

Frequently asked questions

How much water does the adiabatic module use?

Around 4.7 litres per hour per 10,000 m³/h of air and kelvin of cooling. Because the module only runs on hot days, annual consumption remains moderate.

Does it create spray mist or aerosols?

The module works without spray nozzles and without recirculation: water runs over the evaporation surface once and drains away. We check the hygiene requirements for every project.

What water is required?

Softened or partially demineralised water, connected to the mains via a backflow preventer to EN 1717.

Does the module cost cooling capacity?

We design enclosure and adiabatic module so that the additional pressure drop stays below 25 Pa (target). On hot days, the gain from cooler intake air clearly outweighs it.

Can the module be retrofitted?

Yes. The adiabatic module is intended both for new acoustic enclosures and for retrofitting existing ones.
