The obstacle

Many climate-friendly installations fail not because of technology, but because of noise

Air-source heat pumps, chillers, dry coolers and battery storage stand outdoors, often close to homes, schools or hospitals. If the noise limits cannot be met, the unit is throttled, relocated or never built – and the gas boiler stays.

  • Permits: The night-time limit often decides whether a heat pump is possible at a site at all.
  • Operation: Without noise control, units run in reduced night mode – with less capacity and lower efficiency.
  • Low frequencies: The hum of compressors and fans travels far and penetrates walls and windows. Conventional absorbers would have to be very thick to deal with it.
  • Our contribution: An acoustic enclosure with HSA3 makes the site possible – and the unit can run at full capacity.

Energy in operation

Less resistance for the air, less power for the fans

Every acoustic enclosure adds resistance that the unit's fans have to overcome – hour after hour, over the entire service life. HSA3 attenuates with a small component thickness, leaving more free cross-section.

Up to 40 % lower pressure drop

In ABSORA's comparative measurement: 12 instead of 20 Pa at the same sound power as a mineral-wool splitter (key data).

Slim wall build-up

Designed with CFD simulation and a maximum air velocity of 5 m/s. Inside the enclosure the unit works almost as in open air.

Quieter in hot weather

With the adiabatic module, intake air is pre-cooled on hot days. The fans do not have to ramp up, and the chiller stays efficient and quiet (adiabatic module).

Material and life cycle

An absorber made of metal – from manufacture to dismantling

Conventional absorbers consist of mineral wool or foam, combined with fleece, foil or perforated sheet. What remains at the end is mixed waste. HSA3 works purely through its geometry – the absorber itself is metal.

1 · Manufacture

Workshop instead of site

Panels and tubes are prefabricated and assembled in a modular system of slotted profiles. The enclosure weighs only around 13 kg/m² of envelope – on many existing roofs no additional steel structure is needed.

2 · Operation

Weatherproof and durable

HSA3 does not absorb water, contains no fibres that could shed and is weather-resistant. Near the coast in seawater-resistant aluminium or stainless steel.

3 · Maintenance

Clean instead of replace

After a leak or fire, mineral wool and foam must be replaced. HSA3 can be cleaned and reused.

4 · End of life

Back into the metal cycle

The enclosure is bolted and slotted, not glued. It can be modified, adapted to a new unit, or separated by material and recycled.

Frequently asked questions

What makes HSA3 more sustainable than mineral wool?

HSA3 absorbs sound through its geometry, not through fibres. The absorber itself is therefore metal: weatherproof, non-combustible, cleanable and recyclable by material at the end of its life. On top of that, the lower pressure drop saves fan power in operation.

How does an acoustic enclosure contribute to the heat transition?

Many heat pumps are never built or are throttled at night because noise limits cannot be met at the site. An acoustic enclosure makes installation possible and allows operation at full capacity and efficiency.

Does the enclosure cost the unit efficiency?

Every enclosure causes some pressure drop. We design enclosures with CFD simulation and a maximum air velocity of 5 m/s to keep it low; HSA3 silencers have up to 40 % less pressure drop than mineral-wool splitters.

Doesn't the adiabatic module use a lot of water?

The module only operates above 28 °C outdoor temperature, i.e. on hot days only – typically a few hundred hours a year in Central Europe. Demand is around 4.7 litres per hour per 10,000 m³/h of air and kelvin of cooling. We calculate for each unit whether pre-cooling pays off.

Is HSA3 recyclable?

Yes. HSA3 is made of aluminium or stainless steel. Because the enclosures are bolted rather than glued, the materials can be separated and returned to the metal cycle – or the enclosure is modified and reused on a new unit.
