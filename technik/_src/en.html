Questions about HSA3

Why does HSA3 work without mineral wool?

The micro-perforated top layer and the chambers behind it form many small resonators (Helmholtz principle). Air friction in the fine holes turns sound energy into heat. A deliberate difference in phase and sound pressure between front and rear chamber – an impedance jump – creates the absorption. The multi-layer core distributes the rest before it reaches the carrier plate. There are no fibres that soak up moisture or shed.

How does HSA3 compare with conventional insulation at low frequencies?

Porous absorbers such as mineral wool are effective over a broad band, but need greater thickness or suitable cavities for low frequencies. How well they resist moisture and ageing depends strongly on material, facing and construction – which is why, depending on the project, we also use moisture-resistant alternatives such as Stratocell Whisper. HSA3 works through its geometry: locally resonant structures specifically damp the range where compressors, fans and pumps drone. The result: slimmer, lighter enclosures with less pressure drop and therefore more unit capacity.

Is HSA3 suitable for BESS and outdoor use?

Yes. HSA3 is non-combustible (class A1 to EN 13501-1, the highest classification) – especially important for battery storage. The metal is weather-resistant and designed for permanent outdoor use, where chillers and heat pumps are usually installed.

Does it always have to be a full enclosure?

No. Because HSA3 is modular, individual noise sources can be treated: the compressor, the tonal fan noise (blade passing frequency) or the whine of inverters. The modules sit close to the source, can be removed individually and save space and material compared with full cladding – ideal for retrofits.

How is the supporting structure built?

From anodised aluminium industrial profiles 45 × 45 mm with T-slot, joined with standard corner brackets and slot nuts – bolted, not welded. Every part is available from stock, so short lead times instead of one-off fabrication, with no paint line and no weld inspection. The design is proven for self-supporting frames over 12 m and originated in 2015 with the Silent-Mode and ForCoTech brands.

Why is airtightness so important?

Low frequencies find every gap. That is why we seal frames with sealant rather than tape and avoid vibrations. What is laborious in a prototype becomes reproducible in series production – and therefore standard in our custom builds too.

How does an HSA3 project run and how is the effect verified?

From a single source: acoustic design from first consultation to detail, made-to-fit manufacturing of the HSA3 components, site logistics and installation by regional, quality-assured teams – and finally an on-site (in situ) differential measurement that proves the effect.

Air flow decides where the noise goes

With air-cooled units the discussion is often only about dB(A) – not about how the unit draws in and discharges air. Yet that is exactly what decides where the noise goes and where a measure actually works.

Vertical discharge

Fan noise goes upwards, compressors radiate sideways. Critical for upper floors and taller neighbouring buildings.

Table design

Air enters from below. Part of the noise is reflected by the roof surface and escapes sideways.

Inverted table design

Discharge downwards: noise spreads flat in all directions – with a risk of air short-circuit.

Horizontal discharge

Air and noise go straight out. In front of a wall or in a corner, up to 6 dB are added.

Ducted

Only the openings radiate outwards – duct silencers are most efficient here.

AHU with heat recovery

Exhaust and outdoor air both radiate and must be separated against short-circuit.

One rule applies to all: every silencer costs pressure. If the pressure drop rises, the fan speeds up – and the noise level rises again. That is why we always design acoustics and air flow together.

Technology and design

An enclosure must never choke the equipment. We treat it as a coupled acoustic, aerodynamic and thermal system: air volume balance, pressure drop, fan curve, heat exchange at the outdoor unit and acoustics are designed together and verified before anything is built.

  • CFD simulation of air flow and pressure drop – recirculation zones, flow separation, local velocity peaks and thermal short-circuits are minimised, supported by separate intake and discharge chambers
  • Noise calculation from sound power, distance and type of area
  • BIM model for coordination with designers and site management
  • Structural design and wind load to EN 1991-1-4, also for rooftop installation
  • Easy maintenance: doors and removable panels for service and replacement

Target values before detailed design

  • Air velocity in the free openings typically 5–7 m/s as a guide value – the permissible value is checked per project against geometry and fan reserve
  • Noise reduction at the receiver, e.g. 10–15 dB(A), decisive for night-time operation
  • Loss of unit capacity due to the enclosure limited per project to approx. 3–5 % max.

Example: 100,000 m³/h at 5 m/s requires about 5.6 m² of free area – on both the intake and the discharge side.

Safety

Noise control for units with flammable refrigerants (R290)

An enclosure around an R290 unit is feasible – but only with written approval from the unit manufacturer and a design in which leaked propane can flow away freely at the bottom. The German BWP guideline is generally critical of enclosing a unit afterwards; it is only justifiable if the manufacturer confirms the specific solution and the installation and safety concept is updated.

Our standard design – no contact with the unit, no floor, HSA3 inner lining in class A1 – already meets the basic structural requirement. Manufacturer approval, zoning and verification of the openings are added per project. The final design is always based on a project-specific risk assessment and the applicable requirements, in particular EN 378 and national regulations.

No floor, no pit

Propane is about 1.5 times heavier than air and flows like water. The enclosure stays open at the bottom, with no sills or recesses.

No ignition sources

No lights, sockets, switches or drives inside the enclosure – unless explosion-proof for the defined zone.

Openings that cannot be closed

Ventilation and relief openings work even in storms, snow and with the fans at standstill.

Storm and relief openings

  • Ground gap: free all round, minimum height agreed with the manufacturer; falls outwards, never towards light wells, drains or roof outlets. On flat roofs the parapet must not dam the outflow.
  • Cross-ventilation: openings on at least two opposite sides, no motorised louvres – if needed acoustically, only fail-open and wind-pressure-proof. A manufacturer's fan purge must not be obstructed.
  • Pressure relief: if an explosion zone lies inside the enclosure, relief areas to EN 14994 – the open roof air outlet is the natural relief area.

Why a closed envelope is dangerous

Propane is non-toxic but extremely flammable and heavier than air. Example: a double enclosure of 12 × 4.2 × 3.9 m (approx. 197 m³) reaches the lower explosive limit with only about 7.5 kg of propane evenly distributed – and locally much sooner as it layers at the bottom. Large heat pumps contain several times that amount.

Conflict with acoustics

Every ground opening is a sound bridge. Our solution: a surrounding acoustic skirt set at a distance, or a shallow labyrinth lined with HSA3 – always falling outwards. We check air flow and gas outflow with CFD.

Two-stage ATEX concept: passive and in case of a leak

The passive measures – no floor, no pit, no ignition sources, openings that cannot be closed – always work, even during a power cut. For a leak we add a gas detection system that uses the unit’s own fans: instead of a separate Ex ventilation system with ducts and extra fans, all it takes is sensors, a gas detection controller and a warning device.

How the emergency ventilation works

  • Floor-level gas sensors (infrared, Ex version) where propane would linger longest – usually at the closed wall facing the building.
  • Pre-alarm: the controller outside the Ex zone switches the unit fans to 100 %. Outdoor air flows in at floor level through the silencers, exhaust air leaves vertically through the roof silencer, away from façades and windows.
  • Main alarm: compressors off, fans keep running, beacon and horn at the access, signal to the BMS.
  • Fallback: if the manufacturer does not approve fan operation during a leak, we add one Ex exhaust fan per enclosure (ATEX II 2G) with floor-level extraction and vertical discharge away from the building.

Worked example (estimate)

Group enclosure around three large heat pumps with approx. 138,000 m³/h air flow each. Design leak: the content of one refrigerant circuit (7 kg) in 4 minutes. With the unit fans at 100 %, the average concentration in the exhaust air stays below 1 % of the lower flammability limit (0.038 kg/m³) – the target is 25 % at most. The emergency ventilation for machinery rooms to EN 378-3 would be exceeded many times over.

Written approval by the manufacturer for 100 % fan operation during a leak and an interface for shutting down the compressors. Zoning, design leak and alarm thresholds are set by the operator in the explosion protection concept.

Checklist for planning, installation and operation

Planning

  • Written approval of the unit manufacturer for the enclosure
  • Safety zone and refrigerant charge taken from the manufacturer's documentation
  • Zoning defined by operator or expert (explosion protection document)
  • Safety zone does not reach neighbouring plots, paths, car parks or building openings
  • No light well, drain or roof outlet in the outflow path
  • Ground openings on at least two sides, cross-section agreed with the manufacturer
  • CFD verification of air flow, wind influence and gas outflow
  • Relief areas to EN 14994 if a zone lies inside the enclosure
  • Leak concept: gas detection, manufacturer approval for 100 % fan operation, compressor shutdown

Installation

  • Enclosure without floor, no contact with the unit
  • No plinths, sills or trays; falls outwards
  • Electrical equipment only explosion-proof for the zone
  • Enclosure frame bonded to equipotential bonding (agreed with electrical design)
  • No louvres in storm openings, or only fail-open and wind-pressure-proof
  • Inner lining A1 (HSA3), absorbers at least B1
  • Marking: flammable refrigerant, no ignition sources
  • Floor-level gas sensors, gas detection controller outside the Ex zone

Operation and maintenance

  • Check ground and storm openings for leaves, snow and objects at every service
  • No storage in or next to the enclosure
  • Work on the refrigerant circuit only with a gas detector and spark-free tools
  • Test and calibrate the gas detection system regularly
Sustainability and certification

Noise control that makes heat pumps and refrigeration plants possible

Our acoustic enclosures allow air-source heat pumps and refrigeration plants – chillers, condensers, dry coolers – to be installed where noise limits would otherwise rule them out or force reduced operation at night. The unit can run at full capacity and efficiency.

Natural refrigerants

R290 units are enclosed safely: an open base and flow-through openings in the profiles prevent refrigerant from accumulating (details).

Low pressure drop

Slim wall build-up with HSA3 absorbers, engineered with CFD simulation and a maximum air velocity of 5 m/s.

Circular by design

Modular build from slotted profiles, bolted rather than bonded, fully demountable and reusable.

Materials

  • Predominantly recyclable aluminium
  • Sound absorption without mineral wool or foams
  • Profiles and absorbers made of non-combustible material
  • Seawater-resistant aluminium for coastal sites – long service life

LEED, BREEAM and DGNB

Our enclosures can therefore contribute to evidence for LEED, BREEAM and DGNB certification, particularly for energy efficiency, refrigerants and circularity. Certification always applies to the building; the assessor decides on a project basis which credits are achieved. Material and construction data for your documentation come with the quotation.

Circularity of the enclosure

  • Largely single-material metal construction: Frame profiles and panels are made of aluminium. The HSA3 internal lining is available in aluminium or stainless steel and is non-combustible (A1). No mineral wool and no foams are used.
  • Modular, demountable build: The enclosures are bolted and plugged, not bonded. They can be dismantled, modified, adapted to a new unit or reused.
  • Recovery: At the end of their service life the materials can be separated by type and returned to the metal cycle.
