What is HSA3?

A metal that swallows sound – through its shape

Classic absorbers rely on mass and fibres: mineral wool or foam, as thick as possible. HSA3 takes a different route. As an acoustic meta-material it draws its effect from geometry – from many small, precisely tuned chambers, holes and springs.

HSA3 was developed and patented by ABSORA GmbH in Berlin. Silent Engineering has used it for years as the standard in all ForCoTech and Silent-Mode enclosures – as inner lining, as tube silencer and as partition wall.

The result: a lining that also damps low tones, does not burn, does not absorb water and offers the air less resistance than conventional splitters.

Working principle

Three mechanisms in one layer

For HSA3 to work across a broad frequency range – from the compressor's hum to the fan's rush – several physical effects work together.

Helmholtz resonators

The micro-perforated face layer forms many small resonators with the air chambers behind it. Air rubs in the fine holes – sound energy turns into heat.

Mass-spring system

The spring-mounted intermediate layer vibrates with the air in the cavity. This system is tuned to low frequencies, where porous absorbers would have to be very thick.

Impedance step

Between front and rear chamber, a deliberate difference in phase and sound pressure arises. This step creates additional absorption; the multi-layer core dissipates the rest.

Reverberation room data

Sound absorption coefficient to EN ISO 354

In February 2026 ABSORA had HSA3 panels and HSA3 sound pockets tested in several build-ups in the reverberation room of TU Berlin; measurement and evaluation by BeSB Schalltechnisches Büro. All results in one-third octave bands from 100 to 5000 Hz.

  • Low frequencies: HSA3 (0.1) with mineral wool already reaches an absorption coefficient of 0.83 at 100 Hz and 0.93 at 160 Hz – with a build-up depth of only 200 mm. Porous absorbers alone would need several times that thickness.
  • Strongest across the band: HSA3 (0.3) with mineral wool stays at 0.9 and above from 160 to 500 Hz, NRC 0.85, absorber class C.
  • Entirely fibre-free: the fibre-free HSA3 build-ups reach up to 0.80 (315 Hz) and NRC 0.60–0.65 – the choice for R290 units and battery storage, where neither fibres nor combustible materials are allowed. L-profiles additionally raise absorption below 250 Hz and above 1 kHz.
  • HSA3 sound pockets: NRC 0.75 and absorber class C when placed densely, only NRC 0.55 at half coverage – the arrangement matters.

Measured on a project: Berlin-Adlershof

In an existing acoustic enclosure we replaced the XPS intermediate ceiling with HSA3 and measured the sound power before and after – one fan at 100 % speed.

  • Low frequencies 50–100 Hz: 10 to 30 dB less – exactly where compressors and fans hum.
  • Overall level, unweighted: 59.4 instead of 73.6 dB(Z), i.e. about −14 dB.
  • A-weighted almost unchanged: A-weighting gives little weight to low tones. Yet low-frequency hum travels far and passes through walls and windows – that is where HSA3 works.
  • Two fans in operation: HSA3 instead of Stratocell gave 74.7 instead of 77.6 dB(Z).

Measurement commissioned by Silent Engineering, 2026. Single measurement on one installation; the effect depends on unit, enclosure and installation.

Frequently asked questions about HSA3

What does “acoustic meta-material” mean?

Meta-materials get their properties not from the material itself but from their structure. In HSA3 these are periodically arranged chambers, holes and springs that are tuned to the frequencies of compressors and fans.

Why does HSA3 have less pressure drop than classic splitters?

Porous splitters must be thick to work at low frequencies and therefore narrow the free cross-section. HSA3 achieves its effect at low component thickness – more area is left for the air. Compared with conventional splitter silencers, pressure drop falls by up to 40 %.

How are the values verified?

The absorption of HSA3 was tested in the reverberation room of TU Berlin to EN ISO 354; the measured values are shown above on this page and the test report is available as a PDF download. For every project we calculate a noise prediction in advance; after installation an on-site differential measurement proves the actual effect.

Can HSA3 withstand rain, frost and UV?

Yes. HSA3 is a metal, weatherproof and designed for permanent outdoor use, where heat pumps and chillers stand. It does not absorb water and contains no fibres that could shed.

Is HSA3 suitable for units with R290?

Yes. As a non-combustible A1 material, HSA3 meets the requirement for the inner lining of enclosures around propane units. The enclosure itself must also be floorless and approved by the unit manufacturer.

Can HSA3 be used without a full enclosure?

Yes. Because HSA3 is modular, individual noise sources can be treated specifically, such as the compressor compartment or the fan. This saves space and material and is well suited to retrofits.
