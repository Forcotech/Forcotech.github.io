The challenge

On hot days, everything comes together

Air-cooled chillers work at their limit when the outdoor air is at its warmest. That is exactly when the fans run at full speed – and the unit is at its loudest.

  • High pressure: At 35 °C and above, condensing pressure rises. The unit throttles back or trips on fault.
  • Capacity: Cooling capacity and efficiency drop just when demand is highest.
  • Noise: Full fan speed means the highest sound level, often on warm summer nights too.
  • Space: There is usually no room left on site for a separate pre-cooling system.
How it works

The enclosure pre-cools the intake air

The acoustic enclosure already guides the air through a defined intake area. That is where the adiabatic module sits: water evaporates on a wetted surface and draws heat from the incoming air before it reaches the unit’s coil.

Evaporation

Cooling without refrigerant

Evaporating water lowers the air temperature. At 35 °C and 30 % relative humidity, approx. 6–9 K of cooling is possible.

Hygiene

Once-through, wetted not sprayed

Treated reverse-osmosis water runs over the evaporation surface once and then to drain: no recirculation, no spray mist, regular flushing cycles.

Frequently asked questions

What is the hygiene concept, and how is Legionella avoided?

Legionella multiply mainly in standing, warm water and are transmitted via aerosols. The module is designed to avoid both: water runs once over the surface and then to drain, without recirculation. The surface is wetted, not sprayed, so no spray mist is formed. In addition, the water is treated by reverse osmosis and the system runs regular flushing cycles. We check the requirements for evaporative systems (e.g. German 42nd BImSchV, VDI 2047) for every project.

Does it create spray mist or aerosols?

No, the module works without spray nozzles: the evaporation surface is wetted, water runs over it once and drains away.

What water is required?

Mains water, connected via a backflow preventer to EN 1717. A pump unit with reverse osmosis treats it before it reaches the evaporation surface.

How much water does the adiabatic module use?

Around 4.7 litres per hour per 10,000 m³/h of air and kelvin of cooling. Because the module only runs from 28 °C outdoor temperature, annual consumption remains moderate.

How is scaling avoided?

Reverse osmosis largely demineralises the water, so practically no limescale builds up on the evaporation surface. The flushing cycles also keep pipes and surface clean.

Is the material suitable for RO water?

Yes. The HSA3 surface is approved for operation with reverse-osmosis water; in the wet zone we use seawater-resistant aluminium.

When does the module operate – and what happens in winter?

Pre-cooling only starts at 28 °C outdoor temperature, when high-pressure trips and capacity losses become likely. Below that and in winter, the enclosure runs dry like a normal acoustic enclosure. Frost protection of the water-carrying parts is designed for each project.

Why is the unit quieter on hot days with the module?

At high outdoor temperatures, chillers increase fan speed to hold the condensing pressure and become louder as a result. With pre-cooled intake air, this speed increase is not needed. This is a real advantage whenever the hottest day is the critical load case in the noise assessment.

Does the module cost cooling capacity?

We design enclosure and adiabatic module so that the additional pressure drop stays below 25 Pa (target). On hot days, the gain from cooler intake air clearly outweighs it.

Can the module be retrofitted?

Yes. The adiabatic module is intended both for new acoustic enclosures and for retrofitting existing ones.
